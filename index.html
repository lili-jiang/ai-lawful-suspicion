01 / Project overview

When algorithms raise suspicion, evidence matters.

Public agencies may use data-driven systems to identify cases that warrant closer examination. In welfare fraud detection, such systems can affect people’s access to essential support, making the basis for a suspicion consequential.

Lawful Suspicion examines how legal standards can inform the design of AI-supported detection. The project focuses on connecting legal requirements with statistical behaviour so that system decisions can be scrutinised, tested and—where feasible—formally constrained.

Core questionHow can an AI system support fraud detection while making its assumptions, error risks and relationship to legal standards transparent and accountable?
01

Legally grounded

Clarify which legal standards are relevant and how they should shape system requirements.

02

Statistically explicit

Make error rates, uncertainty, thresholds and assumptions open to examination.

03

Auditable and accountable

Support meaningful review of how a system produces and communicates suspicion.

02 / Research objectives

Connecting legal principles to technical properties.

The objectives below translate the project’s stated ambition into a clear research agenda. They can be refined to match the approved project plan.

OBJECTIVE 01

Translate legal standards

Identify relevant legal concepts and translate them into explicit, reviewable requirements for AI-assisted welfare fraud detection.

OBJECTIVE 02

Analyse statistical implications

Study how modelling choices, decision thresholds, uncertainty and different kinds of error relate to those requirements.

OBJECTIVE 03

Make systems auditable

Develop ways to document and inspect system behaviour, assumptions, data limitations and decision pathways.

OBJECTIVE 04

Explore formal guarantees

Investigate which properties can be bounded, verified or otherwise controlled mathematically, and state limitations clearly.

OBJECTIVE 05

Examine fairness and impact

Assess how errors and system design can affect people and public institutions, including the need for meaningful human oversight.

OBJECTIVE 06

Enable responsible practice

Develop concepts, methods or guidance that help researchers and practitioners evaluate AI systems in high-stakes public services.

03 / Research approach

From legal standard to testable system.

This conceptual diagram illustrates one possible research workflow. It is a communication aid, not a claim that a particular method or deployed system has already been validated.

The process is iterative: statistical findings may expose ambiguity in a requirement, while legal analysis may change which system properties need to be measured or controlled.
